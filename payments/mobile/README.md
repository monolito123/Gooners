# goonersPAY merchant apps

The merchant app for Android and iOS: a phone becomes a payment terminal (card and Pix), shows what the store has to receive, sends payouts by Pix, and the owner's phone manages which phones may sell. The design is in [docs/05](../docs/05-mobile.md); this folder is the first working version.

| | |
|---|---|
| [`conformance/vectors.json`](conformance/vectors.json) | Test vectors generated by the platform (`cd ../platform && go run ./cmd/conformance`). Both cores and the spec checker reproduce them byte for byte. CI fails if they are stale |
| [`android/core`](android/core) | Kotlin core (JVM library): money, NIP-01 events and BIP-340, NIP-19, NIP-98 login, DPoP proofs, the kind 17710 device list, BR Code checks, the sandbox card format, the checkout state machine and the API client |
| [`android/app`](android/app) | Jetpack Compose app (Android 11+) |
| [`ios`](ios) | `GoonersPayKit`, the same core as a Swift package (CryptoKit, swift-secp256k1); it builds and tests on Linux and macOS |
| [`ios/App`](ios/App) | SwiftUI app (iOS 17+), generated as an Xcode project by XcodeGen |

## What the apps do

1. **Pairing.** A partner (or the owner's app) creates a single-use enrollment code for a merchant (`POST /v1/merchants/{id}/enrollment-codes`). The phone reads it with `GET /v1/enrollment`, generates its keys and enrolls with `POST /v1/enrollment`. On the merchant's first phone it also creates the **merchant root key** and shows it once for the owner to write down (docs/03 §4.1). A phone never holds an API key.
2. **Login.** Every 15 minutes the phone signs a NIP-98 event (kind 27235) over the exact login request with its **device key**. The session it gets is bound to a **DPoP key** (RFC 9449) that never leaves secure hardware, and works only on the merchant-app routes, for this merchant and this phone's terminal (docs/02 §3.2).
3. **Selling, in two taps (docs/06 §5).** The keypad (with quick amounts), then Cobrar opens the method sheet with the **last method first** and the only filled button; a **long press on Cobrar repeats the last method** in one tap. Credit takes parcelas (1× unless the cashier picks another). A card goes through the reader and `POST /v1/terminal/authorizations`; Pix shows a dynamic QR whose CRC and amount the app checks before the customer sees it; **crypto** (opt-in in Ajustes) shows the exact USDT amount, network, address and countdown, and the merchant receives reais. A payment the platform holds while compliance checks who sent it shows as **Pagamento em verificação**: neither approved nor declined, no tone, one calm next step (docs/09 §6.5). Approval draws a check in 400 ms with the success haptic and two rising notes; a decline shakes once with a double pulse and one low note (the approval tone can be set to play in silent mode, in Ajustes); with reduced motion they cross-fade. The keypad **comes back by itself after 4 s** (a bar shows the time; tapping the receipt QR keeps it). A wrong sale is undone with **Cancelar venda**: a card sale is reversed, a Pix sale refunded to the payer; for a crypto sale the app explains how to return crypto to the customer's address (docs/09 §3.2). Every amount is read to screen readers as words ("cento e vinte e nove reais e noventa centavos"), and every error says what happened, what it means for the money and what to do.
4. **Money.** "Disponível agora" first, with its one action (Sacar por Pix); receivables as a timeline ("amanhã", "28/11"), held and reserved amounts in plain words, entries in the merchant's terms, and a Pix payout: a quote first (fee, net and the quote's countdown), then a fresh biometric confirmation, then the payout.
5. **Ativos (docs/09).** Crypto held (with its value in reais), the USD, EUR and GBP accounts with the details to receive from abroad one tap down (and the reference to copy), B3 positions with their result and pending settlements, and buying or selling crypto or foreign currency: the price, fee and IOF before any commitment, then a biometric confirmation.
6. **Devices (owner).** The owner's phone adds a cashier phone by scanning (or pasting) its `npub`: the root key signs a newer device list (kind 17710) naming both. Removing a phone signs a list without it, which ends its sessions and revokes its terminal at once.
7. **Journal.** Each phone keeps a hash-chained journal (kind 7714, docs/03 §7): a session start at every login, and `tap_started` and `auth_result` for every sale (the receipt links to the tap). Entries chain as they are written, because an entry's ID depends only on the device's public key; they are signed in a batch whenever the device key is unwrapped for a login, and uploaded. The platform reports the entries it lacks and freezes the terminal of a forked journal.
8. **Fraud shield.** Each tap carries the phone's own risk score (docs/07 §3): posture (debugger, root or jailbreak, hooking frameworks, emulator, overlays, screen recording, ADB and developer options) and the till's behaviour (amount-to-tap time, cancelled taps, repeated amounts, bursts, decline streaks), scored by the `gp-device-1` model compiled into both cores. Only the score and at most five signal codes leave the phone. A sale the platform holds says so on the approval screen.
9. **Statements.** Each day becomes a signed statement, gift-wrapped to the merchant's keys, whose receipts prove their way to the day's public transparency root (`GET /v1/settlements`, docs/03 §6). The Money screen lists them; opening one checks it on the phone, trusting nothing else the API says: the platform's signatures (and, in release builds given `goonerspay.platformKeys` / `GOONERSPAY_PLATFORM_KEYS`, that the signers are the platform's published keys), the totals against the lines, the commitment, and the statement's RFC 6962 inclusion in the signed public root of its day. Each check is shown on its own.

## Keys on the phone

| Key | Android | iOS | Used for |
|-----|---------|-----|----------|
| DPoP (P-256) | Keystore, StrongBox when present; never exportable | Secure Enclave (CryptoKit) | Every request's proof |
| Device (secp256k1) | Random, wrapped by a Keystore AES-256-GCM key that needs biometrics or the device credential (30 s window) and is invalidated by a new fingerprint | Random, ECIES-encrypted to a Secure Enclave key that needs Face ID (`biometryCurrentSet`) or the passcode | Signing logins; unwrapped only for the signature and wiped after |
| Merchant root (secp256k1) | Same as the device key, owner's phone only | Same | Signing device lists |
| App Attest key | — | Generated by `DCAppAttestService`, attested with the device key as client data | Proving the genuine app on a genuine iPhone, then signing every card authorization (an assertion over the exact request body, with a counter that only goes up) |

**Attestation.** The Android DPoP key is generated with an attestation challenge bound to the device's Nostr key, and its Keystore certificate chain goes with the enrollment; the iPhone sends App Attest evidence. When the platform verifies attestation (production), rooted phones and cloned or re-signed apps are refused, a phone the owner adds attests at its first login (the apps retry the login with their evidence when told `attestation_required`), and an Android phone's sessions are pinned to its attested DPoP key.

**Certificate pinning.** Release builds pin the API host's keys: pass the SPKI hashes of its current and backup keys, in OkHttp's `sha256/<base64>` form, with `./gradlew -Pgoonerspay.pins=sha256/…,sha256/… :app:assembleRelease` and `xcodebuild GOONERSPAY_PINS='sha256/…,sha256/…'` (the host defaults to `api.goonerspay.com.br`; override with `goonerspay.pinnedHost` / `GOONERSPAY_PINNED_HOST`). A trusted chain that holds none of the pins is refused before any request leaves the phone. Without pins there is no pinning, as in the sandbox.

Both apps refuse to pair a phone without a screen lock in release builds, keep secrets out of backups, block screenshots (Android) or hide the screen in the app switcher (iOS), and refuse redirects so a session token never follows a 3xx elsewhere. Release builds speak HTTPS only.

Android also hides other apps' overlays from its window (Android 12+) and notes touches that reach it through one.

Card reading is behind one interface (`CardReader`). Production plugs in the licensed MPoC SDK on Android and ProximityReader (Tap to Pay on iPhone) or a PCI PTS reader on iOS; they encrypt inside their secure boundary and the app only forwards the blob. Until those contracts exist, the only reader is the **sandbox reader**: it "taps" scheme test cards in the development format the sandbox decrypts, which the platform refuses in production. Each reader holds its own copy of each test card (the test card's BIN and last four digits, random digits between) with its own transaction counter, so the fraud shield's card rules see one customer's card rather than a number every sandbox user shares.

## Run it against the sandbox

```bash
# 1. The sandbox: platform, card scheme and Pix PSP simulators, on a fresh database.
cd payments/platform
DATABASE_URL=postgres://… go run ./cmd/smoke -serve /tmp/sandbox.json
# /tmp/sandbox.json now holds api_url, a partner api_key, dev_card_key and a merchant.

# 2. Tests of both cores, including the whole protocol against that sandbox.
cd ../mobile/android && GOONERSPAY_SANDBOX=/tmp/sandbox.json ./gradlew :core:test
cd ../ios && GOONERSPAY_SANDBOX=/tmp/sandbox.json swift test
```

The integration tests create a merchant and an enrollment code with the partner key, then enroll a phone, log in, sell by card and by Pix, pay out, add a cashier phone and remove it. Without `GOONERSPAY_SANDBOX` they are skipped and the conformance tests still run.

**Android app.** JDK 21 and the Android SDK (platform 37). `./gradlew :app:assembleDebug` builds `app/build/outputs/apk/debug/app-debug.apk`. On an emulator the default platform address is `http://10.0.2.2:8080` (the host machine); debug builds allow cleartext to it. Create an enrollment code (`POST /v1/merchants/{id}/enrollment-codes` with the partner key), type it in the app, and paste the sandbox's `dev_card_key` to enable the sandbox reader.

**iOS app.** Xcode 16.3 or later and [XcodeGen](https://github.com/yonaskolb/XcodeGen) 2.46: `cd ios/App && xcodegen generate && open GoonersPay.xcodeproj`. The simulator has no Secure Enclave, so there the keys are kept in the keychain unwrapped (development only); run on a device to use the Enclave and Face ID.

## Status and next steps

Built and tested: pairing, the root key backup, NIP-98 login with DPoP sessions, attestation evidence (verified by the platform when it has the roots), card sales through the sandbox reader, Pix, crypto charges, the two-tap checkout with method memory, long-press repeat, parcelas, auto-return and Cancelar venda, spoken amounts, receivables, payouts with biometric step-up, crypto and foreign-currency conversions, accounts abroad, B3 positions, device management with QR scanning, the terminal journal, certificate pinning, the fraud shield's device score and statements checked on the phone, on both platforms. The checkout's behaviour, the spoken-amount table and the wording table are unit-tested in both cores against the same values. CI (`.github/workflows/payments-mobile.yml`) runs the conformance and sandbox tests of both cores, builds the Android app (debug, minified release, lint) and the iOS app for the simulator and for devices.

Not built yet, in the order they matter:

1. **Real card acceptance**: the MPoC SDK adapter (Android) and ProximityReader (iOS), which need the vendor contract and Apple's Tap to Pay entitlement.
2. **Play Integrity** at session start (it needs Google's decode API on the platform). iPhones already sign every sale with their attested key.
3. **The mTLS terminal certificate** of docs/05 §2.4, issued after attestation.
4. **Offline sales**: static Pix QR and cash sales recorded offline (the journal already queues; the sale screens need the network).
5. Localization beyond pt-BR, and the sunlight theme.
6. B3 orders from the phone (the web console places them today).
